Olist Project – Delivery Performance vs Customer Satisfaction Analysis
This is the most important analysis in your Olist project because you are connecting two business areas:
- Operations: How well does Olist deliver orders?
- Customer Experience: How do customers rate their orders?
Your objective is to investigate whether late deliveries are associated with lower customer ratings.

Below are notes explaining your code step by step.
1. Objective
You want to answer this business question:

Do customers who receive late deliveries give lower ratings compared to customers who receive early or on-time deliveries?

To answer this, you combine the orders dataset with the reviews dataset.
2. Load the Datasets
orders = pd.read_csv(DATA_PATH / "olist_orders_dataset.csv")reviews = pd.read_csv(DATA_PATH / "olist_order_reviews_dataset.csv")

3. Convert Dates into DateTime Format
orders['order_purchase_timestamp'] = pd.to_datetime(    orders['order_purchase_timestamp'],    format='%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S')


You convert the purchase timestamp from text into a proper date/time format.

You do the same for estimated and actual delivery dates.

Why?

Because Pandas can subtract dates only when they are in a date/time format.
Important correction
You are converting the estimated and actual delivery dates twice.

You can remove the duplicate conversions and use:
orders["order_delivered_customer_date"] = pd.to_datetime(    orders["order_delivered_customer_date"], errors="coerce")orders["order_estimated_delivery_date"] = pd.to_datetime(    orders["order_estimated_delivery_date"], errors="coerce")


errors="coerce" converts invalid or missing dates into NaT (Not a Time).
4. Calculate Delivery Delay
delivery_delay = (    orders["order_delivered_customer_date"]    - orders["order_estimated_delivery_date"])orders["delivery_delay_days"] = delivery_delay.dt.days


Formula
Delivery Delay = Actual Delivery Date − Estimated Delivery Date

Example:
Estimated Date	Actual Date	Delay
10 November	13 November	3 days
10 November	10 November	0 days
10 November	7 November	-3 days


Interpretation:
- Positive value = Late delivery.
- Zero = On-time delivery.
- Negative value = Early delivery.
- Missing value = Delivery date unavailable.
.dt.days extracts the number of days from the date difference.
5. Select Required Columns
delivery_data = orders[["order_id", "delivery_delay_days"]]reviews_data = reviews[["order_id", "review_score"]]


You select only the columns needed for your analysis.

6. Merge Both Datasets
delivery_reviews = delivery_data.merge(    reviews_data,    on="order_id")

Now you can study delivery performance alongside customer ratings.

By default, merge() uses an inner join when you don't specify how. It keeps matching order IDs from both datasets.
7. Check Missing Values
print(delivery_reviews.notna().sum())


- notna() identifies values that are not missing.
- sum() counts the available values in each column.
You then filter records with available delivery delays:
valid_delivery_review = delivery_reviews[    delivery_reviews["delivery_delay_days"].notna()].copy()


This keeps records where the delivery delay is available.

.copy() creates a separate copy of the filtered data.

Why is this important?

You cannot calculate delivery delay for orders that do not have an actual delivery date.
8. Calculate Average Customer Rating Per Order
order_reviews = reviews.groupby("order_id").agg(    average_rating=("review_score", "mean"),    review_count=("review_score", "count")).reset_index()


This is one of the most important Pandas concepts in your project.

Let's understand each function.
Function	Meaning
groupby()	Groups records with the same order ID
agg()	Performs calculations on grouped data
mean()	Calculates average rating
count()	Counts review records
reset_index()	Converts the grouped order ID back into a regular column


Why calculate average rating per order?
Some orders have multiple review records.

If you compare all review records directly, an order with multiple reviews could receive more weight.

Calculating one average rating per order makes the comparison fairer.
9. Merge Delivery Data With Average Ratings
final_data = delivery_data.merge(    order_reviews[["order_id", "average_rating", "review_count"]],    on="order_id",    how="left")


This creates your final analysis dataset.

You use how="left" because you want to keep all orders from delivery_data, even if some orders do not have a customer review.

Your resulting dataset contains:
Column	Meaning
order_id	Unique order identifier
delivery_delay_days	Delivery delay
average_rating	Average customer rating
review_count	Number of reviews for that order


10. Remove Missing Delivery Dates
final_data = final_data.dropna(    subset=["delivery_delay_days"]).copy()


dropna() removes rows with missing values.

Here, you only remove rows where delivery delay is missing.

This ensures that every remaining order can be classified as early, on-time or late.

One more improvement: Before calculating average ratings, also exclude orders without a rating. Otherwise, the delivery groups may have different numbers of orders and reviews.
11. Categorise Delivery Performance
final_data["delivery_category"] = (    final_data["delivery_delay_days"].gt(0).map({        True: "late",        False: "Early/on time"    }))


Let's break this down.
.gt(0)
gt means greater than.

It checks whether delivery delay is greater than zero.

Example:

.map()
map() replaces True and False with meaningful labels.
Boolean	Category

This makes your data easier to understand in Power BI.
12. Compare Average Ratings by Delivery Category
rating_comparison = final_data.groupby("delivery_category").agg(    total_orders=("order_id", "count"),    average_rating=("average_rating", "mean")).reset_index()


This is the final calculation.

You group orders into two categories:
- Late

13. Important Improvement to Your Final Code
Before grouping, I recommend using this small correction:
analysis_data = final_data.dropna(    subset=["delivery_delay_days", "average_rating"]).copy()


Then create the delivery category and calculate the comparison using analysis_data instead of final_data.

Why?

Because an order without a customer rating should not be included in the average rating calculation.

Also, your current final_data may contain multiple rows for an order if that order has multiple review records in the original review dataset. Your grouped order_reviews table avoids that issue by creating one row per order.
14. Business Understanding
Your analysis can help a company understand:
1. Whether late deliveries are associated with lower customer ratings.
2. Whether delivery performance is connected with customer experience.
3. How much attention should be given to delivery operations.
4. Whether improving delivery reliability could be worth investigating as a customer satisfaction strategy.
Remember: Correlation does not prove causation. Your analysis can show an association, but it cannot prove that delivery delays caused lower ratings.
